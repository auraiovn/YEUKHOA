HOW COLOUR CAN SIMPLIFY A LOOK

One strong colour can create direction without adding more pieces.

Colour can make a look feel more complex, but it can also make dressing simpler. The key is to let one colour carry the message and keep the rest of the silhouette controlled.

Choose one lead colour

Orange, pink and cobalt blue are strong enough to become the visual centre of an outfit. If you choose one as the lead, the remaining pieces can stay cream, sand, taupe or ink. This reduces the need for extra detail.

Use accessories as a small colour test

If a full bright set feels like too much, try the colour at a smaller scale. Sunglasses, shoes or a compact bag can create direction without changing the entire wardrobe. This is also a useful way to learn which colours you enjoy repeating.

Check colour near the face

Live camera tools can help because you see the colour in relation to your own face. Lighting still changes the result, so controlled product photography remains the reference for exact product colour.

Repeat a colour with restraint

If you want a more campaign driven look, repeat the lead colour once in a smaller detail. For example, a coral blazer with an orange frame can feel connected. Adding several unrelated bright colours can make the eye work harder than necessary.

Use neutral structure

Bright colour is easier to wear when the garment shape is clear. A simple blazer, straight trouser or clean knit gives the colour enough room to do its job. That is why AURA pairs saturated colour with straightforward silhouettes.
